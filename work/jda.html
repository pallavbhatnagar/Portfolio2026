Overview

A self-initiated concept for an infrastructure firm whose real site listed services but hid its most impressive work several pages deep.

The problem

Prospective clients wanted evidence: bridges, stations, towers. The site opened with a mission statement and a list of capabilities.

“Show me something you've built that looks like my project.”

Procurement lead, informal interview

What we learned

Proof sells

Buyers compared firms by past projects, not by service lists.

Scale needs context

A photo of a bridge meant more with its span, budget and timeline beside it.

Contact is a hand-off

Serious enquiries wanted a named person, not a general form.

Process

  1. 01

    Listen

    Short conversations with three people who buy infrastructure services.

  2. 02

    Map

    Reorganised the site around projects instead of departments.

  3. 03

    Sketch

    A bold, image-led layout with the facts that buyers asked for.

  4. 04

    Design

    High-fidelity pages for the home, a project story and contact.

  5. 05

    Share

    Presented the concept as a case for leading with proof.

Key decisions

Projects on the first screen

The home page opens on a finished project, with the next one a swipe away.

Facts beside every photo

Span, budget and timeline sit next to each image, so scale reads instantly.

A named person to contact

Each project ends with the lead who ran it and a direct way to reach them.

Results

The concept became a talking point with the studio I shared it with, and the project-first structure shaped a later client pitch.

Looking back

I'd test the project-first home page against the old one with real visitors before calling it better.
