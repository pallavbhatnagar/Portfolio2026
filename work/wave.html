Overview

Traditional brokerage firms were losing customers to digital-first brokers with slicker, cheaper apps. 63 Moons built Wave 2.0 so any broker could launch a modern trading app under its own name, and our team at THEM Consulting designed the experience from the first workshop to the final build.

The market

New-age brokers were winning with better technology, low or zero fees and apps that made trading feel easy. Their large, growing user bases gave them an edge that older firms couldn't match with their existing tools.

The business goal

Established brokers wanted a competitive, future-ready mobile app with a genuinely better experience, to win new investors faster and keep the ones they already had.

The product

Wave 2.0 is a white-label trading app. Each broker rebrands it and switches features on or off to match its own services, so it launches a polished app in weeks instead of building one over years.

Who we designed for

Wave 2.0 had two sets of customers: the brokers who buy and brand it, and the traders who use it every day. We designed for traders first, because their trust and activity is what brokers were paying for.

Just starting out

Has money to invest and a lot of curiosity, but no idea which stocks to pick or where to start.

Needs

  • Plain language instead of jargon
  • Proof that others are doing it too

Gets in the way

  • Building a watchlist from nothing
  • Fear of making an expensive mistake

Trades most days

The active trader

Watches the market through the day and acts quickly on news, alerts and price moves.

Needs

  • Speed from idea to order
  • Live context next to every decision
  • Alerts that are worth opening

Gets in the way

  • Hopping between screens to place one trade
  • Missing a move while searching the app

Years of experience

The seasoned investor

Researches carefully, trades options and manages a diverse portfolio.

Needs

  • Deep research without the busywork
  • A clear, scannable option chain
  • Control over every order detail

Gets in the way

  • Research spread across tools and tabs
  • Dense tables that are slow to read

What we learned

We spoke with brokerage firms, active traders and the 63 Moons sales team, who hear what brokers ask for every day. Brokers needed to reinvent their image with digital experiences, and a better experience had to show up in their numbers: more transactions, more engagement, more customers who stay.

What moves people to trade

Synthesising the research in a product envisioning workshop, we kept coming back to human motives rather than features. Five shaped the product more than anything else:

Fear of missing out

People act when they see gains they might miss, especially when a stock is trending or friends and influencers are profiting from it.

Confidence from experience

A good first experience and a better understanding of the market make people more willing to trade again. Early positive returns build momentum.

Fear of loss

Avoiding a loss matters more than chasing a gain. People hold onto losing stocks or spread their money out to feel safe.

Personal goals

Building wealth, planning for retirement or saving for something specific gives people a reason to invest and keep investing.

Money arriving

A bonus, a salary hike or any large sum landing in the bank is a common moment when people decide to put money to work.

Where existing apps let traders down

Reviewing competitor apps alongside the research showed the same gaps again and again. Each one was an opportunity for Wave 2.0.

No hand-holding

Apps didn't match how people actually decide. New users were left to figure everything out alone.

Too much to remember

Screens demanded constant attention and memory, from stock symbols to order states, which made people hesitate before trading.

No sense of the market

Order statuses, market updates and news lived on separate screens, so every decision was made with part of the picture missing.

Missed opportunities

With little guidance on what to buy, people struggled to choose and often didn't act at all.

Guesswork instead of insight

Without clear, contextual guidance, the fear of making a mistake grew. Informed decisions build confidence; guesswork erodes it.

Confusing navigation

Deep, rigid menus made it hard to find the right screen, especially during time-sensitive tasks like placing or changing an order.

What we set out to achieve

We agreed five product goals with 63 Moons and used them to judge every design decision, from the smallest interaction to the navigation.

Drive engagement and transactions

Make trading feel effortless so people trade more often. This is where brokers earn their revenue.

Win and keep customers

Give new investors an easy start and give existing ones reasons to stay instead of switching apps.

Easy to customise

A modular product that every broker can brand, and switch features on or off, without redesigning it.

Guidance that builds trust

Explain what's happening at the moment it matters, so people trade with confidence rather than anxiety.

Easy to find your way

Put the most important actions within reach and make the rest easy to discover.

Design principles

Three principles kept a large team consistent. When two good ideas competed, the one that served these better won.

Trust and reliability

Money is involved, so every screen has to feel dependable.

  • Consistent patterns everywhere
  • Clear feedback for every action
  • Prevent errors, and let people undo
  • Social proof where it helps

Power and confidence

Help people feel in control of their decisions.

  • Simple, human language
  • Less ambiguity and confusion
  • Information that supports the decision
  • Room to learn and explore

Fast and actionable

In a moving market, a few seconds decide the outcome.

  • Surface time-sensitive opportunities
  • Important actions within reach
  • Recognition over recall
  • Context that adapts to the person

Rebuilding the navigation

We listed every action a trader takes and mapped them on two questions: how often do people do this, and how much does it matter when they do? The top-right corner became the heart of the app; the rest moved out of the way.

Frequent, high-stakes actions got the shortest paths and a place on the main screens. Occasional but important tasks stayed one clear step away, and everything else moved into a lighter secondary layer.

Making investing smarter and more actionable

Our strategy was to lower the barriers to each decision, then give people good reasons to come back. These are the decisions that mattered most.

Start with a ready-made watchlist

Choosing stocks was the first big hurdle for new users. They can now pick a watchlist curated by experts, or create their own, and be ready to trade in minutes.

  • Easier start
  • More first trades
  • Higher confidence
  • Better acquisition

Opportunities come to you

New events and opportunities from across all watchlists surface at the top of the screen, so traders stop switching between lists to find what's moving.

  • Recognition over recall
  • Fewer taps
  • Faster decisions
  • More trades

Trade right from the list

Buy and sell sit on the watchlist itself, with just enough context to act with confidence. The app flexes to different trading styles instead of forcing one path.

  • More conversions
  • Trust and reliability
  • Ease of use

Building a habit, not just a feature

Getting someone to trade once is not enough. We designed small, useful reasons to come back every day:

Content that changes

Curated watchlists, opportunities, recommendations, news and research refresh through the day, so there's always something new worth checking.

Alerts that prompt action

Price alerts and portfolio milestones reach people on their lock screen at the moment they can act on them.

Widgets on the home screen

The things people care about stay visible outside the app, which keeps the market, and the app, top of mind.

An app that adapts

The market screen learns what each person follows and puts it first, making it harder to switch to a competitor.

Research without the rabbit hole

Researching a stock usually meant hopping between tools. We brought indices, top gainers, news and search into one market screen, so seasoned investors can go from a hunch to an informed trade without leaving the app.

Options trading, re-thought

Options are where experienced traders spend the most time, and where existing apps were the hardest to read. We redesigned the option chain around how people scan it:

Existing apps

  • Dense tables with every column at once
  • Calls and puts hard to tell apart
  • The current price lost in the list
  • Several screens to place one order

Wave 2.0

  • Calls and puts side by side around the strike price
  • The current price marked where the eye expects it
  • Only the columns that matter, the rest on demand
  • Trade straight from the chain

One design system, every broker

Because Wave 2.0 is sold to many brokers, the design system is the product as much as the screens are. The Moon Design System grew to more than 250 components and made designing new features about four times faster.

Fast design and delivery

Designers and developers assemble new mobile and web screens from ready-made components, and it's still how new features are designed and built today.

Modular and easy to update

A flexible structure absorbs new features and changes, and lets each broker adapt any component to its own needs.

Adapts to each brand

Colours, contrast and type adjust to a broker's brand from a single set of tokens, without breaking accessibility.

Built for one codebase

The system follows the Ionic framework's structure, so one build serves iOS and Android and developers and designers speak the same language.

What I took away

Wave 2.0 was my first project at this scale, and it changed how I work.

Speed and polish can coexist

I kept improving the product as I understood it better, but not everything could be solved in one release. Shipping the best possible version within the limits, then improving it, beat waiting for perfect.

Ambiguity doesn't go away

I started out wanting research and testing to answer everything. Some questions stay open, and a reasoned call based on experience, tested quickly, is often the right move.

Design for value, not features

Product teams naturally want to add more. The pain points, context and behaviour behind a feature decide whether it's worth building, so I learned to ask the hard questions early.

Teams make each other better

The best work came when we understood each other's constraints and made it safe to share problems early. That openness is what let a team this size move fast.
