Overview

Ledgerly helps young professionals budget without spreadsheets. The app worked, but new users rarely reached the moment it became useful: most left during onboarding, before they had linked an account or seen a single insight about their spending.

The problem

Onboarding asked for everything up front: income, goals, categories, three linked accounts. People were being asked to plan a budget before the app had shown them anything about their own money. The first screen they reached afterwards was a dashboard of empty charts.

“I didn't know what my categories were. That's why I downloaded the app.”

Participant 4, first-time user

What we learned

Show before you ask

People were happy to link an account once they saw what they'd get back. Asking first felt like paperwork.

Categories are a result, not an input

Nobody could name their spending categories from memory. They recognised them instantly once the app suggested them.

Calm beats complete

The overview tried to show everything. Users wanted one honest number: what's left this month.

Process

  1. 01

    Listen

    Twelve interviews and a diary study with new users over their first week.

  2. 02

    Map

    A journey map of the first ten minutes, marking every point someone gave up.

  3. 03

    Sketch

    Paper flows with the team, then three competing onboarding prototypes.

  4. 04

    Test

    Three rounds of moderated tests, changing one thing per round.

  5. 05

    Ship

    A staged rollout to 10% of new users, then everyone once the numbers held.

Key decisions

Link one account, then show the payoff

Onboarding now asks for a single account and immediately shows last month's spending, grouped for you. Everything else can wait.

Suggested categories you can correct

The app proposes categories from real transactions. You rename or merge them with a tap instead of building them from scratch.

One number at the top

The overview leads with what's left to spend this month. Charts moved one tap deeper, for the people who want them.

Results

Within a month of the full rollout, more new users finished onboarding and most of them set a budget on day one. Support tickets about "missing categories" all but disappeared.

Looking back

I'd bring engineering into the diary study earlier. Two of the best ideas came from an engineer who read the raw notes, and we could have had them weeks sooner.
