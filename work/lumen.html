Overview

Lumen Learn offers short online courses for working adults. Sign-ups were healthy, but most students never finished their first lesson.

The problem

The first screen offered forty courses, six filters and a quiz. Students spent their motivation choosing and had little left for learning.

“I spent twenty minutes picking a course and then had to leave for work.”

Student, round one

What we learned

People arrive with a goal

Almost everyone could say why they signed up in one sentence. We never asked.

The first win matters most

Students who finished one five-minute lesson were far more likely to come back.

Progress needs a shape

A list of forty videos felt endless. A short path with a finish line felt doable.

Process

  1. 01

    Listen

    Interviews with students who signed up but never finished a lesson.

  2. 02

    Map

    Traced the path from sign-up to first completed lesson, minute by minute.

  3. 03

    Sketch

    Replaced the catalogue with a single question: what do you want to be able to do?

  4. 04

    Test

    Three rounds of usability sessions with real students.

  5. 05

    Ship

    Piloted with two partner companies before the public launch.

Key decisions

One question instead of forty courses

Students state a goal and get one recommended path, with the full catalogue a tap away.

A first lesson you can finish today

Every path starts with a five-minute lesson designed to end in a small, real win.

A path with a finish line

Progress shows as a short route with a clear end, not a long list of videos.

Results

Completion rose across both pilot companies, and the usability score climbed in every round of testing.

Looking back

The goal question worked so well that I'd test making it the whole home page.
