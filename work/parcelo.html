Overview

Parcelo helps regional courier firms plan and track the day's deliveries. Dispatchers kept the dashboard open all day, yet still found problems through phone calls from angry customers.

The problem

Every order looked equally important. A delivery running two hours late sat in the same grey row as one that was on time, sorted by order number.

“By the time I see it on screen, the customer has already called me.”

Dispatcher, Leeds depot

What we learned

Dispatchers scan, they don't read

Eye-tracking showed people sweeping the left edge of the table for anything unusual.

Late is a spectrum

Ten minutes late and two hours late needed different actions, not the same red badge.

Fixes happen by phone

Most problems ended in a call to a driver, but the number lived in another system.

Process

  1. 01

    Listen

    Sat with dispatchers through two full shifts, noting every interruption.

  2. 02

    Map

    Listed every action taken on a late order and how long each one took.

  3. 03

    Sketch

    Rebuilt the table around urgency, then tested three ways to show lateness.

  4. 04

    Test

    Timed dispatchers finding and fixing planted late orders.

  5. 05

    Ship

    Shipped behind a toggle so teams could switch back during the first week.

Key decisions

Sort by urgency, always

The most at-risk orders sit at the top, and the list re-sorts itself as the day moves.

Lateness you can read at a glance

A slim bar on the row's left edge grows with delay, so a sweep of the eye finds trouble.

Call the driver from the row

The driver's number and last position open in place. No switching systems.

Results

Teams resolved late deliveries in half the time and several depots dropped their separate spreadsheet for tracking problem orders.

Looking back

We measured speed but not stress. I'd love to learn whether the calmer screen changed how the end of a shift feels.
